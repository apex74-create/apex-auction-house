House rules

How a drop works · how buying works

The drop

  • Every sale runs as a timed drop: a lot hangs on the floor with its valuation published ahead, and offers are taken while it hangs.
  • The queue hears drop dates first and holds first-refusal order when a lot goes unsold.
  • An offer at or above reserve that the house accepts takes the lot off the floor.
  • A lot that does not sell returns to the reclaim pile — reworked and re-dropped later at a re-measured price. Never air: every turn of this crank passes through a real buyer paying real money for a real, finished property.

The purchase

  • Offer — by email to the house (the button on the lot). Questions are answered before money is discussed; the house would rather lose an offer to a clear answer than win one on a misunderstanding.
  • Written agreement — the sale is written down in plain words: what transfers, what is licensed, the turns included, the price.
  • Cleared payment — transfer happens on cleared funds, through the rail named in the agreement.
  • Transfer — the domain moves to the buyer's registrar account and the property's code repository is handed over working. The engine is licensed, and the buyer holds the licence — keyed to them, at the published card.
  • Running costs are the buyer's from day one: hosting (the storefront is static files and runs on free tiers), the domain renewal at the registrar's standard rate, and traffic. The house promises no traffic and no income, on any lot, ever.
The Amber Law, house-wide: any money matter on this floor — a reserve, a fee, a balance — is stated in calm amber and plain words: this needs your attention; nothing is broken, and nobody's dying. Red is reserved for genuine danger in this house. It is never a sales lever.
