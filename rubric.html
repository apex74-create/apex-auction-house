THE RUBRIC ON THE WALL

How this house values what it sells — published, so any bidder can grade our homework.

Why it is published

A rubric is the red ink that tells you how the grade is given

A published methodology can be checked, argued with, and built to. An unpublished number is just weather somebody makes up. So this house publishes its method on the wall of the floor: the signals the Valuation Desk reads, the discipline it cuts the number with, and — at the bottom, in the same size print — the honest limits of what any estimate means.

The desk's method stands on two legs. For a bare name, it reads the same published signals the market's own valuation model (GoDaddy's GoValue) reads — because the venues' tools hunt those same signals. For a built property, it prices off real, fetched market comparables for the property's class, under Guestimator law: band the comparables by weight class, average them, and cut the reserve at the bottom end of the average — with every figure labelled an ESTIMATE.

The signals

What the desk reads on a name

SignalWhat it means
Comparable salesWhat similar names actually sold for. The anchor every model trains on — and the reason the first sale of a name family is never fired cheap: it becomes the comparable its siblings are read against.
Keyword value & popularityReal words with commercial intent read higher than invented strings. "Air solutions" is a trade phrase; "money saver" is an exact-match consumer phrase.
Extension (the TLD).com carries the strongest default reading — with the model's own counterweight printed beside it: an extension alone does not determine a name's value.
LengthShorter reads stronger; the published line is under 15 characters. Names over the line compensate through usage and demand evidence, below.
Semantics & brandabilityWhat the words mean and how they work together — could this name stand over a door? A semantic layer now reads exactly that.
ScarcityHow rare the name or its characteristics are in the market. A name family held in one hand is scarcity the holder owns.
Web presence — the buildable oneKeyword usage, extensions in use, and company usage: a name a real company actually trades under, on real pages, reads differently from a parked string. This is the signal a builder can honestly move.
Market trendsRising or falling interest in particular terms and extensions.
MemorabilityWhether the name survives being heard once, in a parking lot, over a radio.
Age & backlinksNamed by the model's publisher for its valuation tool. Age cannot be built — only protected: a lapsed name loses its age, its stack position, and its signals in one stroke.
Demand evidenceNot part of the sticker — the venue-side file that travels with it: inbound offers, views, searches, connected analytics a buyer can verify in one sitting.

Sources: GoDaddy's published appraisal methodology and GoValue descriptions (godaddy.com/resources), read November 12, 2026. The house's full working sheet — the estate scorecard and build rules — is the Valuation Desk's build sheet, kept with the wing's papers.

The discipline

How a reserve is cut on this floor

  • Real comparables, fetched, never remembered. A reserve cites the sales it was cut from.
  • Bottom end of the average. The hoped-for ceiling is not the floor: pricing the reserve at the top of the band thins the room that has to form under it. If the room wants to pay more, the drop is how it says so.
  • Lines shown separately. When a package carries a bracket (a second stocked category), the bracket prices as its own line — never blended invisibly into the base.
  • An unmeasured thing is priced NO MEASURE, not guessed. Where a property has no honest comparable class, the desk says so and prices it when the catalogue is whole.
  • Appraisal stickers are anchors, never price bases. A registrar's estimate of a bare name is a reference point for the name — it does not price the built property standing on it.

Worked example — Lot № 1. The apexairsolutions.shop storefront package: nine real website-market comparables (pre-revenue and done-for-you classes) banded $900–$9,997, mean ≈ $3,699; bottom-end cut lands the reserve at $2,999, labelled ESTIMATE, with an expected clearing band of $3,700–$5,000 on the strength of the 24-in-12 supply term. With the extension bracket folded in (+$2,000 as its own line), the combined reserve reads $4,999 — and a clean-title variant (no engine licence) reads $4,499, the premium pricing the surrendered meter. The full sheet stands with the wing's papers.

Honest limits

What an estimate is not

  • An estimate is a reading, not a bid. Nobody owes it. No buy-back, no guaranteed sale, and no brokerage at the estimate exists behind any number on this floor — the same is true of the registrar's own appraisals, whose publisher disclaims any guarantee in its own text.
  • The models miss in both directions. Independent testing records a name appraised at $2,615 that sold for $15,000, and one appraised at $171 that sold for $8,000. The desk steers by the signals because buyers verify the same evidence — not because the sticker is scripture.
  • Building to the rubric steers the reading and the presentation. It does not manufacture a buyer. The proof of the whole exercise is venue-side: offers, views, closes.
  • The strongest rubric lines are the no-regrets lines — real doors on real names, real content in the trade's own words, connected analytics, protected renewals, honest listings. They pay even if no model ever moves a dollar.
