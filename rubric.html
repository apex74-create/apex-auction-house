HOW WE SET PRICES

Real sales of similar properties, averaged — and we take the low end.

The method

How a reserve is set

For a built property, we look up real sales of similar properties, average them, and set the reserve at the bottom of the average. For a bare domain name, we read the same signals the market's own valuation tools read: what similar names sold for, the words in the name, its length, and its ending. Every price on this floor is an estimate.

Name signals follow GoDaddy's published appraisal method, read November 12, 2026.

Worked example

Lot № 1, priced

Nine comparable website sales: $900–$9,997, average about $3,699. The reserve is the low end: $2,999 — an ESTIMATE. The clean-title version, with no engine licence, is $4,499 — also an ESTIMATE.

Honest limits

What an estimate is not

  • An estimate is our best reading, not a bid. Nobody owes it, and no sale at that price is guaranteed.
  • Appraisals miss in both directions. A name appraised at $2,615 once sold for $15,000; one appraised at $171 sold for $8,000.
  • A price does not make a buyer appear. Buyers decide.
